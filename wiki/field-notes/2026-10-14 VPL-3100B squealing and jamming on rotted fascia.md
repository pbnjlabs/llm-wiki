# 2026-10-14 — VPL-3100B: squealing and getting stuck (delaminating plywood fascia)

**Status: resolved (closed 2026-10-20, per the human).** PMS service
call on a [[VPL-3100B]] at a deck. **The problem isn't specific to one
make:** it applies to any VPL running past a plywood fascia. Job date
assumed to be today (not confirmed).

> **Key takeaways (per the human):**
> 1. **Not make-specific.** Any VPL with a **plywood fascia** can do
>    this.
> 2. **Plywood met all the requirements at install, but 6 years later
>    it's delaminating** and the platform jams on it.
> 3. **Fix: remove the existing fascia and rebuild it with treated
>    decking boards.**

## Unit

- Bruno [[VPL-3100B]], **serial 20060350524**, **installed 2020** at a
  deck. Original installer not recorded (per the human, "whoever
  installed the VPL used plywood").
- Fascia (the wall the platform runs past between the landings):
  **plywood**.

## Symptom

- Reported: **"making a squealing noise."**
- On test: the noise **could be recreated**, and the **lift was getting
  stuck**.

## Checked

- Ran the lift: recreated the squeal and found it sticking.
- Closer inspection: the **platform was jamming on the deck's fascia**.

## Root cause

**The plywood fascia was delaminating** (rotting and buckling) after 6
years, so it pushed into the platform's path. That caused the
squeal and the jamming. The plywood met all the requirements when it
was installed in 2020 (per the human).

## Fix

Removed the plywood fascia and **rebuilt it with treated decking
boards** (per the human). The human closed the job on 2026-10-20; the
completion date and the running-clearance check weren't recorded.

## Parts used

Treated decking boards (quantity not recorded).

## Takeaways

1. **Not make-specific: any VPL with a plywood fascia** (per the human).
2. **Plywood that met the requirements at install can delaminate within
   about 6 years** and jam the platform (per the human).
3. **Rebuild with treated decking boards** (per the human).

From the manuals (not field experience). The rebuilt fascia still has to
meet them:
- **Bruno:** the fascia must be **smooth, vertical and structurally
  sound**, and **smooth and non-perforated** over the full length and
  width of the platform. Fasten it from the upper landing sill down to
  the lower landing sill. It must withstand a **125 lb side load over
  any 4 in square**. ASME A18.1 requires **3/8–3/4 in (10–19 mm) running
  clearance between the platform and the fascia**. After the work, run
  the platform up and down to verify that clearance
  ([[VPL-3100B Install Manual]], pp. 7, 9–11; the
  [[VPL-3200B Install Manual]] says the same). Follow local codes.
- **Harmar:** fascia panels must give a **smooth surface for the
  platform edge to run against**, with no overlaps or gaps. A deck with
  an opening underneath needs a fascia wall, and you may need to stud up
  the wall to fasten it. Harmar sells fascia panels: 53 or 24 in high,
  43 or 49 in wide, custom heights on special order
  ([[Highlander II Install Manual (no enclosure)]], p. 20).
