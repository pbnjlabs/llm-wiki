# 2026-10-14 — Harmar outside lift: stuck lifting a load (J-cam flat spots)

**Status: resolved (known issue and fix).** PMS field experience on
Harmar vehicle lifts. The manuals don't cover it. Not tied to one job.

> **Key takeaways (per the human):**
> 1. **The J-cam can develop flat spots over time.** Seen on **all the
>    models**.
> 2. **Symptom: the lift gets stuck lifting a load. Without a load
>    there's no issue.**
> 3. **Same spot every time.** The lift appears to work fine until it
>    hits the flat spot. A **bad actuator or low power** makes the lift
>    **slow the whole way up** instead. Don't confuse them.
> 4. **You can usually feel the flat spot on the heel of the J-cam.**
> 5. **Fix: replace the J-cam, part 140-00075.**
> 6. **Clean the J-cam with a dry 3M Scouring Pad (Synthetic Fiber).
>    Never lube it.**

## Unit

- Harmar hitch-mounted outside vehicle lifts
  ([[AL-Series-Outside-Vehicle-Lifts]]). **Seen on all the models** (per
  the human). In the manuals on file, the J-cam appears only on the
  outside lifts: [[Outside Vehicle Lifts Owner's Manual]] Figure 4-1
  (p. 13) shows it on the **mast just above the platform**, a
  spring-loaded cam with the platform roller just below it. The AL100
  and AL301XL assembly drawings list it ([[Harmar Parts Diagrams]]).
- Serial number: not recorded (general note, not one job).

## Symptom

- **Gets stuck lifting a load** (mobility device on the platform), at
  the **same spot every time**.
- Works fine up to that point. **No issue without a load.**

## Checked

How to tell it from the look-alikes (per the human):

| | J-cam flat spot | Bad actuator or low power |
|---|---|---|
| **Lifting a load** | Normal speed, then **stuck at the same spot every time** | **Slow the whole way up** |
| **Without a load** | No issue | — |
| **By hand** | Flat spot usually **felt on the heel of the J-cam** | — |

## Root cause

**Flat spots worn into the J-cam over time** (per the human).

## Fix

- **Replace the J-cam: 140-00075.**
- **Clean the J-cam with a dry 3M Scouring Pad (Synthetic Fiber).
  Never lube it.**

## Parts used

- **J-cam: 140-00075** (per the human).
- That number isn't in any manual on file. The AL100 and AL301XL
  assembly drawings list the J-cam as **530-00716, "J-CAM, CASTED"**,
  which replaced **H11010** (AL100 in 2019; AL301XL in 2021, after
  switching back and forth) ([[Harmar Parts Diagrams]]). Order
  140-00075, the part PMS uses.

## Takeaways

1. **J-cam flat spots develop over time, on all the models** (per the
   human).
2. **Stuck lifting a load at the same spot every time, fine without a
   load: J-cam.** Slow the whole way up: actuator or power (per the
   human).
3. **Feel the heel of the J-cam** for the flat spot (per the human).
4. **Replace the J-cam: 140-00075** (per the human).
5. **Clean the J-cam with a dry 3M Scouring Pad (Synthetic Fiber); never
   lube it** (per the human).

From the manuals (not field experience):
- Harmar's outside lift troubleshooting covers only power, not the
  J-cam. The motor draws up to 20 A, so a meter or test light can show
  12 V through a bad connection. Test current as well as voltage, or run
  the lift from a known-good, fully charged battery
  ([[Outside Vehicle Lifts Install Manual (2025, Rev D)]], Tech Note:
  Motor Testing and Section 7).
- The owner's manual says to oil pivot points on all moving parts every
  6 months. Figure 4-1's oil can points at the **platform roller**, not
  the J-cam, so this doesn't conflict with the human's rule: **don't
  oil the J-cam.**
