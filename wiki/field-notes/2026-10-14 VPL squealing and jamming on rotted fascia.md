# 2026-10-14 — VPL: squealing and getting stuck (rotted plywood fascia)

**Status: open (cause found; fix and takeaways not reported yet).** PMS
service call on a vertical platform lift (porch lift). Job date assumed
to be today; confirm.

> **Key takeaways (per the human):** not given yet.

## Unit

- Vertical platform lift at a **deck**, **installed in 2020**. Make,
  model and serial number: not recorded.
- The deck's **fascia** (the wall the platform runs past between the
  landings) was **plywood**.

## Symptom

- Reported: **"making a squealing noise."**
- On test: the noise **could be recreated**, and the **lift was getting
  stuck**.

## Checked

- Ran the lift: recreated the squeal and found it sticking.
- Closer inspection: the **platform was jamming on the deck's fascia**.

## Root cause

**The plywood fascia was rotting and buckling**, so it pushed into the
platform's path. That caused the squeal and the jamming.

## Takeaways

Key takeaways (per the human): not given yet.

From the manuals (not field experience):
- **Bruno:** the fascia must be **smooth, vertical and structurally
  sound**. Confirm its structural integrity before installing. ASME
  A18.1 requires **3/8–3/4 in (10–19 mm) running clearance between the
  platform and the fascia**. The fascia must be smooth, non-perforated,
  cover the full length and width of the platform, be securely fastened
  from the upper landing sill down to the lower landing sill, and
  withstand a **125 lb side load over any 4 in square**
  ([[VPL-3100B Install Manual]], pp. 7, 9–10; the
  [[VPL-3200B Install Manual]] says the same).
- **Harmar:** fascia panels must give a **smooth surface for the
  platform edge to run against**, with no overlaps or gaps. A deck with
  an opening underneath needs a fascia wall, and you may need to stud up
  the wall to fasten it. Harmar sells fascia panels: 53 or 24 in high,
  43 or 49 in wide, custom heights on special order
  ([[Highlander II Install Manual (no enclosure)]], p. 20).
