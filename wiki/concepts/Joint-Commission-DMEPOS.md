# Joint Commission DMEPOS Standards: what they mean for PMS

**PMS adheres to the Joint Commission DMEPOS standards** (per the human,
2026-10-12). This is a PMS house standard: it applies to every delivery,
setup, repair and service job. Full text (139 standards, 487 EPs):
[[Joint Commission DMEPOS Standards (2026)]]. Standard numbers below are
Joint Commission's, so they can be looked up in that extract. This page
is a field summary. **The extract is the authority.**

Many of these EPs require that PMS **follow the manufacturer's
guidelines**. That is why the wiki's manuals matter, and why a missing
manual is a compliance gap as well as an inconvenience (see below).

## Manufacturer documents (EQ.01.03.01 EP 1)

PMS must **obtain from the manufacturer copies of the features,
warranties and instructions for each type of item it supplies**. When a
device's manual isn't on file, the tech should get a copy (wiki rule:
see [[Ingestion-Backlog#Missing manuals]]). Training must also follow the
manufacturer's instructions (PC.02.03.01 EP 15).

## Delivery (EQ.01.01.01, PC.02.01.03)

- Supply only items that meet FDA and device safety standards.
- Tell the patient the expected delivery time frame, and deliver within
  the time agreed with the patient and prescriber.
- **Secure equipment during transport** to prevent damage and staff
  injury.
- Deliver equipment that is **sanitary, clean and undamaged**.
- Make sure the item matches the patient's needs and limits, and
  **matches the prescriber's order** (PC.02.01.03 EP 11).
- **Verify and document delivery** (signed ticket, call, tracking slip).
  See [[PMS-Office-Procedures]].
- Medicare: offer equipment for trial when needed; verify items aren't
  counterfeit or misbranded, and document it.

## Setup in the home (EQ.01.02.01)

- Provide every supply needed to run the equipment. Unpack, assemble,
  and do **safety and operational checks**. Fit and adjust it to the
  patient. Store it correctly.
- **Check that the home can use the equipment safely:** outlets,
  extension cords, grounding and circuit overload; heat and open flame;
  liquids; emergency power (generator or charged backup battery); and
  the equipment's electrical and environmental needs.
- Identify safety and security risks in the home and act on them. In a
  home, that may be limited to educating the patient (EC.02.01.01 EP 2–3).
- **Fall risk:** assess it, act on it, and teach the patient
  (NPSG.09.02.01).

## Patient training (PC.02.03.01)

- Give **written and verbal** instructions on use, maintenance, hazards
  and safety (EP 11). Cover setup, features, routine use,
  **troubleshooting, cleaning and maintenance** (EP 12), and infection
  control for the equipment (EP 13).
- Tailor the training to the patient's abilities, language and the
  item's risk and manufacturer instructions. **Check that they
  understood** (EP 25).
- Tell the patient the business hours, how to reach PMS, and **who to
  contact after hours** (PC.02.02.05).
- Tell the patient how to raise safety concerns (EP 27) and how to file
  complaints (RI.01.07.01).
- Pride/Quantum chairs and scooters: the owner's-manual care, charging
  and check schedule to teach is on [[Pride-Power-Chair-Care]]; the
  consumer safety guides cover inclines, curbs and vehicle transport.

## Maintenance, repair and inspection (EQ.01.03.01)

- Do routine and **preventive maintenance at set intervals, per the
  manufacturer's guidelines**, and document it. If the manufacturer has
  no schedule, PMS sets one (EP 2).
- Do safety, operation and function checks and repairs **per PMS policy
  and the manufacturer**, and document them (EP 3).
  Bruno's maintenance control plans give the schedule and a log format:
  stairlifts [[Stairlift-Maintenance]] (ILS-01446), VPLs
  [[VPL-Maintenance]] (ILS-01444).
- **Inspect equipment between patients** (rentals, loaners, returns)
  and document it (EP 5).
- Medicare: provide **loaner equipment** equivalent to the original
  while a patient-owned item is being repaired (EP 6).
- **Record identification** (manufacturer, model, **serial number**,
  lot) so recalls can be handled (EP 7). **Act on recalls and hazard
  notices** and notify patients, staff and prescribers (EP 8). Field
  notes already identify units by model and serial number.
- Test and measuring equipment PMS uses (meters, scales, slope gauges)
  must be checked per its manufacturer, with records kept (EQ.02.01.01 EP 3).

## Equipment that would hurt the patient if it failed (EQ.01.04.01)

Identify which equipment would threaten life or health if it failed.
For health-threatening equipment, provide backup equipment, repair or
replacement, or arrange it through a partner closer to the patient.
Life-threatening equipment needs **24/7 service** and a backup system.

## Shop storage and cleaning (EQ.01.05.01)

Keep **clearly marked, separate areas** for: obsolete equipment;
equipment needing maintenance or repair; **dirty** equipment; **clean**
equipment; and **patient-ready** equipment. Clean and disinfect in a
**separate designated area**. Store with expiry dates, temperature and
**battery charge requirements** in mind; see [[Mobility-Batteries]].
Keep patient-ready equipment and storage areas clean.

## Incidents, failures and complaints

- Report internally and investigate **equipment problems, failures and
  use errors**, and any incident or injury where equipment may have
  played a part (EC.04.01.01 EP 1, 17, 18).
- Medicare: start the investigation **within 24 hours** if the incident
  caused hospitalization or death, otherwise **within 72 hours** (EP
  19–20).
- Report device-related death, serious injury or serious illness as the
  **Safe Medical Devices Act** requires (EP 21).
- Complaints (Medicare): acknowledge **within 5 calendar days**, give a
  written result **within 14 days**, and keep records (RI.01.07.01).

## Infection control and hand hygiene

Follow standard precautions, including **reprocessing (cleaning and
disinfecting) reusable equipment** (IC.04.01.01), and follow CDC or WHO
hand-hygiene guidelines (NPSG.07.01.01). Educate staff and patients on
infection control.

## Staff (HR)

Define competencies. Assess them **at orientation and at least every 3
years**. **Technical staff must be competent to deliver and set up
equipment, provide services and train patients and caregivers**
(HR.01.06.01 EP 14). Staff take part in ongoing training, which is
documented, including **workplace-violence prevention** training at
hire and yearly (HR.01.05.03).

## Records (RC.02.01.01)

The patient record holds the equipment provided, documents on whether
the home is suitable or adapted, safety measures, education given,
communications, and (Medicare) the **make and model** of noncustom
equipment and the **seating/positioning evaluation** for complex rehab
equipment. Customer information belongs in PMS's patient records, **not
in wiki field notes**.

## Other

- **Not applicable to PMS:** **PMS doesn't supply oxygen** (per the human,
  2026-10-12), so the oxygen and respiratory requirements don't apply.
  These are NPSG.15.02.01 (home oxygen fire risk), EC.02.01.03 EP 4,
  EC.02.01.01 EP 4 (medical gases), EQ.01.01.01 EP 11, EQ.01.02.01 EP 5,
  EQ.01.04.01 EP 4, 5, 8 and 9, PC.02.02.05 EP 2 and PC.02.03.01 EP 16.
  Infusion and medication EPs don't apply either.
- Emergency management, leadership, medication management and
  performance improvement chapters are office and management
  obligations. They're in the extract but not summarized here.
