# Picture Perfect / StairsChecker 3.0 (Staircase Measuring System)

Bruno's dealer-side photogrammetry system used to survey a staircase and
generate the measurements needed to fabricate a **custom curved rail**
(e.g. for [[CRE-2110]]). This is an ordering/design tool, not part of the
stairlift itself — a dealer photographs coded plastic targets placed on
the staircase, and Bruno's StairsChecker 3.0 software validates the
photoshoot (pass/fail) before the job is submitted for rail fabrication.

## Document lineage (same underlying system, 3 files on disk — revisions/pieces, not distinct systems)

- `Bruno Camera Overview.pdf` (ILS-01397, dated 2020-01-20) — older
  revision of the full instructions.
- `Picture Perfect Instructions.pdf` (ILS-01397, **Rev 5, 2022-10-16**) —
  **current** version of the same document (ILS-01397); supersedes the
  2020 file above. Adds Windows 11 / Surface Pro X compatibility notes and
  explicit device incompatibilities (no Apple, Android, Chromebook, Kindle,
  or Ryzen-CPU machines).
- `PICTURE PERFECT STAIRS CHECKER 3.0_STAIRCASE TARGET PLACEMENT_08-30-2019.pdf`
  (ILS-01403, 2019-08-30) — a standalone excerpt of just the "target and
  level placement" diagrams (90°/180° turns, large-radius turns, straights
  with intermediate landing, existing-stairlift-in-place scenarios). Same
  diagrams are also embedded in both files above.

## How it works

- Kit contains: matched pairs of numbered step-nose targets (LH/RH, 1-30
  each), 48 flat measurement/filler targets, 3 landing-level targets
  (lower/middle/upper, each must be level), 1 scale bar, and a dedicated
  digital camera.
- Step-nose target pairs are numbered per riser, starting at #1 at the
  lower landing; only place as many pairs as there are risers (extras
  stay in the kit).
- All 3 landing levels are used on every project regardless of actual
  landing count; each must show all 4 coded corners and be level.
- Shoot from the top down, working in overlapping sections; every target
  must appear in **≥10 photos** from varying angles/orientations
  (~50 photos average per job). A camera roll/rotation habit
  (portrait+landscape) improves target visibility.
- StairsChecker 3.0 software (desktop app) processes the photos, verifies
  scale bar/camera settings/landing placement/target order, and returns a
  pass/fail with specific warnings (e.g. "Step Nose 9 LH not identified")
  before the dealer leaves the customer's home — avoiding return trips for
  reshoots.
- Outdoor/Elite Curve applications have extra constraints: avoid direct
  sun, rain, and wind; cover reflective surfaces; keep steps in a straight
  line where possible to avoid unintended custom bends being designed in.

## Not the same as: Harmar's Helix PhotoMap

`HARMAR Camera Measuring Manual.pdf` is filed in the same `MANUALS/Bruno/
Bruno Curved SL/` folder but documents a **different manufacturer's**
competing product — Harmar's "CSL Series" curved stairlift and its "Helix
PhotoMap" measuring kit. See [[Harmar]]. Harmar's system uses simple
printed paper markers (no coded plastic targets, no local pass/fail
software) and submits photos via Dropbox/email for human review at
Harmar, rather than Bruno's on-site software validation. Likely filed here
because it addresses the same real-world task (photographing a staircase
for a curved rail order) rather than because it's a Bruno product — flag
for the human to confirm whether this belongs in a Harmar folder instead.
