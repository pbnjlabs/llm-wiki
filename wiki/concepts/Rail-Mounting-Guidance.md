# Rail Mounting Guidance (ILS-01512)

Bruno's single cross-model reference document for anchoring a stairlift
rail into different staircase construction types. Titled "SRE-2010 Model
Rail Mounting Guidance," but the identical document (same revision P2,
same date 2022-09-26, same figures) is bundled verbatim into both the
[[SRE-2010 Tech Support Guide]] and the [[SRE-3050 Tech Support Guide]] —
confirmed by direct comparison of both PDFs. Treat it as applicable to any
Bruno straight-rail model, not just the SRE-2010.

## Construction types covered, and whether Bruno-provided hardware works

| Construction | Bruno hardware OK? | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Standard solid wood (5/4" fir/pine) | Yes | No predrilling normally needed; 6.5mm x 50mm self-tapping screws |
| Hardwood (maple, hickory, Brazilian cherry) | Yes | Pilot hole required (3/16" bit) to prevent splitting |
| Engineered wood (composite, post-2010 construction) | Evaluate case by case | Varies by manufacturer; must meet 750 lbf clamp tensile / 50 lb-in torsional minimums |
| Open wooden treads | Evaluate case by case | Pilot hole (3/16"); must understand tread-to-stringer/wall fastening |
| Marble/tile | Evaluate case by case | Underlay must be identified first; special fasteners likely needed |
| Steel treads/landings or steel pans w/ poured cement | **No** | Requires thru-drilling + underside bracket (≥3/16" thick, coated), ¼" Grade 5 / M6 Grade 8.8 through-bolting |
| Poured cement (≥3" thick) | **No** | Special anchors per local code; hammer drill; caution for embedded snow-melt cable/power lines |
| Cement precast | **No** | Thru-drilling + underside bracket, same as steel |
| Cinder block/paving stone/stone/brick/landscape block | **No** (generally not recommended for mounting at all) | Concrete pilings adjacent to stairs are the usual workaround |
| Railroad ties / landscape timbers | Evaluate case by case | Check for internal decay; shim to level |
| Glass/brittle materials | **No** | Requires custom mounting + expert inspection before quoting |

## Universal minimums (from the doc)

- Torsional load rating: ≥50 lb-in/anchor for typical wood treads (don't
  exceed 70 lb-in for other materials)
- Clamp (tensile pull-out) load: ≥750 lbf/anchor
- These were derived via worst-case testing with a 5x safety factor

**Current version:** the standalone [[ILS-01512 SRE Rail Mounting Guidance (2026)]]
(04/30/26) covers the same rules for SRE-2010 (all variants), SRE-3050
and **SRE-5000**, and adds **"Polywood"** (HDPE lumber, polymer or
composite decking treads): no standard wood fasteners, custom mounting
after expert review. It also says indoor SRE-2010, SRE-3050 and SRE-5000
models aren't for outdoor masonry, block or railroad-tie stairs.

## Mounting hardware (Bruno ILS sheets)

From [[ILS - Stairlift Rail Mounting Hardware]] and
[[ILS - Stairlift Rail and Gear Rack Tools]]:
- **1 in rail mount spacer SRE-30545:** moves mounts back so all four
  fasteners bite and adds a **2° setback toward the wall** against lean;
  use the longer screws from its kit on wood.
- Adjustable stand feet; spiral stand feet (**at least 2 bolts**, M12
  50 ft-lb); **tall CRE feet** 14.9–23.9 in (M10 30 ft-lb, M12 50 ft-lb);
  CRE adjustable clamps K, L, M.
- **2021 fabricated stand feet** (supply-chain substitute for SRE-30501):
  don't loosen the factory spacer hardware.
- **Rail cross pin** (kit SRV-3000-023) keeps the gear rack in if the
  bottom end plate comes off; **swage tool** tightens a loose rack.
- **CRE slope:** digital inclinometer on top of the rail, **±0.5°**, every
  2 ft.

## Clamps and rail position (Bruno tech tips)

Older dealer-portal notes; the model's install manual wins:
- **Standfoot orientation is safety-critical** (SRE-2010, SRE-3000): long
  nose toward the centre of the stairs, no gap at the front
  ([[Tech Tip - SRE-2010 Standfoot Orientation]]).
- **Clamp heights:** standard **SRE-30001** is 5.22 in; the adjustable
  **SRE-30150** goes from 6.19 to 9.19 in
  ([[Tech Tip - SRE-3000 Clamp Comparison (Adjustable Stand Foot)]]).
- **Old die-cast clamps corrode** (older SRE-2010E outdoor units, acid
  step-washes or galvanic action): replace them right away
  ([[Tech Tip - SRE-2010E Clamp Deterioration]]).
- **Old-style SRE-2010 rail:** clamps two at the top, two at the bottom,
  one each side of the joint, no more than **two blank treads** between
  clamps; rail **4.5 in from the wall** and at least **2.5 in from the
  step noses** ([[Tech Tip - SRE-2010 Quick Install Guide (Old Style Rail)]]).
- **CRE curved rail:** at least **2½ in from the step nose** too, or the
  carriage/footrest can hit it ([[Tech Tip - CRE Fit Issues Service Presentation]]).
- **Clamp on the riser:** a Bruno training photo shows an adjustable
  clamp bolted to the **face of the bottom riser** instead of a tread, and
  one with rail-height tags for the SRE-3050 (7.5 in), Elite SRE-2010
  (6 in) and SRE-2010C (8 in). The photos have no text. **Per the human
  (unconfirmed):** the tags are how far above the upper landing each
  model's rail reaches with the legs at maximum height, and the riser
  clamp steadies a section of rail
  ([[Training - Straight Rail Photos (Clamp on Riser, Rail Measurements)]]).

## When in doubt

Bruno recommends bringing in a structural/construction engineer to inspect
unusual staircase construction and confirm both structural adequacy and
local code compliance — especially in seismic zones, coastal/salt-air
environments, or areas with frequent vibration (nearby roads/rail).
