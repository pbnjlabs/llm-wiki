# Hitch Adapters (hitch-mounted vehicle lifts)

The **hitch adapter** slides into the vehicle's receiver hitch and holds
the lift's post or mast. It sets the lift at the **right height** (a drop
or raise) and the **right distance from the bumper**. **Every
hitch-mounted lift install needs one, so put it on every estimate**
([[Vehicle Lift Workflow]]).

Applies to Harmar's outside lifts ([[AL-Series-Outside-Vehicle-Lifts]])
and Bruno's [[ASL-250]], [[ASL-275]] and [[ASL-700]] Chariot. Hoist,
truck and inside-platform lifts bolt inside the vehicle and don't use
one.

## The two rules

From Harmar ([[Harmar Product Insights (2021-2023)]]):
1. **The platform must reach the ground and sit level.**
2. **Once it touches the ground it must not keep running and start
   lifting the car.** That damages the lift.

Bruno says the same in its own terms: if the platform sags (the outer
edge hits the ground first), adjust the hitch height so the unit is
level ([[ASL-700 Installation Manual (2025)]]).

## Measure first

| Measurement | Harmar | Bruno |
|---|---|---|
| **Hitch height** | **HV**: ground to the **bottom of the receiver opening**. Round to the nearest inch (½ in or more rounds up) | Ground to the receiver (ASL-700: to the **inside lip**). **Always round up** (ASL-250/275) |
| **Bumper distance** | **D1**: pin-hole center to the rearmost point of the vehicle. **D2**: pin-hole center to the nearest point going forward through the receiver (sometimes the spare tire) | ASL-700: at least **6.5 in** between the bumper and the back of the lift |
| **Hitch class** | Usually stamped on the hitch, or measure the receiver: **1¼ in = class 2, 2 in = class 3, 2½ in = class 5** (class 1 is also 1¼ in, per Bruno) | ASL-250/275: class II (1¼ in) or III (2 in). ASL-700: class I or II, 1¼ in only |

The hitch also has to carry the load: see **Tongue weight** below.

## Choosing the adapter

### Harmar

- **AL1235U universal class 3 drop adapter**, the most common. One
  adapter sets up as **no drop or a 2, 3 or 5 in drop**. With the
  standard AL107 post: no drop up to 18 in, 2 in at 19–20, 3 in at 21,
  5 in at 22–23. With a swing-away (AL105/AL105L): no drop up to 16 in,
  2 in at 17–18, 3 in at 19, 5 in at 20–21. Higher: call Harmar.
  Fasteners **75 ft-lb** ([[Bulletin - AL1235U Universal Drop Hitch Adapter]]).
  A Harmar sales sheet adds a 4 in setting; go by the bulletin.
- **Bigger drops:** **AL1236** 6 in drop (HV 20–24 in standard, 18–22 in
  with a swing-away; HD and class 5 "V" versions); **AL1238** 8 in drop
  (24–26 in).
- **Spare tire on the back:** use an **Extra Long** adapter, which
  reaches 7.5–13.5 in behind the pin hole (D1). Standard adapters reach
  8 in or less. Models AL125HD, AL1252, AL1255HD, AL1256, AL1258HD. A
  **shallow hitch** blocked by a spare tire may need a **short** adapter
  instead.
- The full chart (about 40 adapters by class, HV, D1 and D2) is in
  [[Outside Vehicle Lifts Install Manual (2025, Rev D)]], pp. 10–11. Or
  use **Harmar's Compatibility Calculator**.
- Most current models ship with the AL107 post and an **AL123** class 3
  adapter (AL123HD on 400–450 lb models)
  ([[Harmar Mobility Solutions Chart (2026, Rev F)]]).

### Bruno ASL-275

Adapters come in **standard, 3 in and 6 in drop/raise**, each usable
**up or down**, and the **mast hole** fine-tunes the height. The range
covers hitch heights of **6–24 in** ([[ASL-275 Install Manual]]):

| Hitch height | Adapter, position, mast hole |
|---|---|
| 6–9 in | 6 in, **up**, bottom → top |
| 10–12 in | 3 in, up, third → top |
| 13–15 in | Standard, up, third → top |
| 16–18 in | Standard, **down**, third → top |
| 19–21 in | 3 in, down, third → top |
| 22–24 in | 6 in, down, third → top |

Part numbers: class III **ASL-16604** (standard), **ASL-16605** (3 in),
**ASL-16606** (6 in); class II **ASL-16607**, **ASL-16608**,
**ASL-16609**.

### Bruno ASL-250 (legacy) and ASL-700

- **ASL-250:** a 19-row height table, with and without a swing-away.
  Standard range is 12–19 in without a swing-away and 9–17 in with one,
  extending to 7–25 in and 7–23 in with optional adapters ([[ASL-250]]).
- **ASL-700 Chariot:** a **4-bar linkage** adapter for **8–16 in**
  receivers, in four settings: 1 = 8–9¾ in, 2 = 10–11¾ in,
  3 = 12–13½ in, 4 = 13¾–16 in. Keep the linkage bars as close to
  parallel as possible. Bruno recommends a **frame-rail hitch**, not a
  trunk-pan or floor-style hitch ([[ASL-700]]). Bruno's 2016 spec sheet:
  tongue weight about **50 lb**, vehicle at least **2,400 lb**; it also
  mentions a 2 in receiver with adapter ASL-K-707, which the 2025 manual
  doesn't allow (manual wins)
  ([[Tech Tip - ASL-700 Chariot Specification Sheet (2016)]]).

## Installing

- **Only the maker's adapters.** Harmar: **hitch expanders and
  extenders are strictly prohibited**; aftermarket accessories risk
  vehicle damage and injury. Bruno: don't modify or add a receiver unless
  the vehicle owner's manual allows it or the vehicle has an OEM towing
  package.
- **Harmar:** orient the adapter on the post for the hitch height. Only
  the standard adapters and the class 2 2 in, class 2 5 in and class 3
  2 in drops can be **inverted**. Secure it by hitch class; on class 3 a
  pinch-bolt tube presses the adapter against the receiver wall to
  **stop rattle**. Don't overtighten the post.
- **Bruno ASL-250/275:** put **Magnalube** on all sides of the adapter,
  tighten the **pinch bolt on class III**, and **level the mast to 90°**
  before final tightening. The hardware is stainless: use anti-seize on
  refastened threads and **never use a pneumatic wrench**, which can
  self-weld the fastener. The class II hitch bolt is Grade 5; class III
  hardware is Grade 8.
- **Bruno ASL-700:** power the Chariot up and down to align the adapter
  with the receiver. Pin it at the hole that gives at least 6.5 in from
  the bumper. At the first 10, 25 and 50 miles, after any wheel removal
  and periodically after that, check that the **hitch pin is fully
  engaged and clear of the hitch** and the clevis pin is seated.

## Tongue weight

**Lift + swing-away + mobility device must not exceed the hitch's rated
tongue weight** (Harmar). Bruno puts it as: **tongue weight rating minus
installed lift weight = the heaviest device allowed**. Read the hitch
label and the vehicle's rating. Worked numbers for Golden devices, and
typical class ratings, are on [[Mobility-Device-Weights]].

## Safety bulletin: AL1232 cold welds

**AL1232** adapters from orders received in **December 2015** (invoices
M66000–M66999) may have **cold welds** that can let the adapter separate
from the vehicle. Inspect them, and replace and destroy any with cold
welds. Harmar reimburses 1 h labor plus mileage under CSV-000014
([[Bulletin - AL1232 Hitch Adapter Cold Welds]]).

## On the estimate and order

- **List the adapter's class and drop** on the Harmar order email, with
  the vehicle, device, lift and swing-away ([[Harmar Orders and Installs Workflow]]).
- **No receiver hitch?** PMS orders one from etrailer.com or etowing.com
  ([[Vehicle Lift Workflow]]). Bruno: have a hitch tech install it first,
  with a flat-4 light harness, and have the underbody inspected; rusted
  vehicles aren't suitable.
