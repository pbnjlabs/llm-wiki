# Ceiling Lift Evaluation

How to evaluate a home for a ceiling lift, plan the track, and check
the install. PMS's own questions come from the
[[Ceiling Lift Evaluation Form]]. The track rules come from Vancare's
[[Ceiling Lift Installation Guide]], which covers the Prism C-Series
and P-440 lifts.

## Which lift

| Lift | What it is | Capacity |
|---|---|---|
| [[C-450-C-625]] | Fixed in the track. Powered up/down, with powered or manual travel along the track. Charges at a track end stop or along a charging strip | 450 or 625 lb (C-800 and C-1000 exist, no manuals on file) |
| [[P440]] | **Portable.** Carried by hand from track to track, pushed along the track. Only up/down is powered | 440 lb (P-600 exists, no manual on file) |
| [[Prism-Castor-2-Post-Lift-Stand]] | Free-standing two-post track on castors, for when nothing can be mounted to the house | 450 or 625 lb, by track |

P-440 trolleys fit Vancare, Mikyway, Guldman and ArjoTrack track.

## 1. Patient and use

From the PMS form:

- Patient weight and height. This sets the lift capacity, and that
  capacity becomes the system's **safe working load (SWL)** for the track
  rules and tests below.
- **Amputee?** Above or below the knee, one leg or both. This matters for
  sling choice.
- **Purpose:** bed to chair only, bed to chair **and bathtub**, or other.
  This decides how many rooms and pick-up points the track has to reach.

The Vancare guide adds questions worth asking: short- or long-term use;
the kinds of transfers (bed to chair or stretcher, bed to toilet, gait
training); whether the lift needs to weigh the client; who will run it;
how much support and strength the client has; and their cognitive
abilities.

## 2. House and structure

**Site-built home** (PMS form):

- **Attic space directly above the room?** Then use **top-down**
  mounting, the most common method. Strut across the joists in the attic,
  with threaded rod down through the ceiling to the track brackets.
- **Another room directly above** (a downstairs room in a two-story
  house)? No attic access, so use **bottom-up** mounting.
- New construction or TJI joists: **wood blocking**.
- Nothing above will hold it: **wall mount**.
- Draw the **joist direction** if it's known.

**Mobile home** (PMS form): plan on **transportable motors with
wall-mounted tracks**. Get wall-to-wall room measurements and ceiling
heights, and confirm the ceiling access is found and the attic can be
reached.

The Homecare Hardware Kit isn't rated over **625 lb**. Above that, use
½ in hardware instead of ⅜ in, except on direct mounts.

## 3. Measure and sketch

- **Pick-up points:** every spot the client gets lifted from or set down
  (bed, chair, toilet, tub). **The track has to pass over each one.**
- **Obstructions** along the track path (door frames, ceiling fans,
  shower rods) and under it (furniture, lights).
- **Doorways:** a track through a doorway needs the frame modified by a
  licensed contractor, or a Vancare Transpoint system.
- Room dimensions, ceiling heights, and where the pick-up points are.
- Draw the track: **shortest path, fewest curves**, with pick-up points,
  obstacles, turntables and the charger marked.
- The homeowner signs and dates the form.

## 4. Power

- C-Series chargers plug into a **standard outlet, which doesn't need to
  be dedicated** (100–240 V AC, 29 W max). The charger cord is 8 ft long.
- **Put the outlet within 2 ft of the track end** where the lift parks.
  If the nearest outlet is too far, a licensed electrician adds one near
  the ceiling (see [[PMS Electrical Work Orders and Preferred Electricians]]
  for PMS's electricians).
- Charging: at an **end stop** (the lift docks at one end) or along the
  whole track (**Omni**, a continuous TransStrip charging rail).

## 5. Track rules

Every section of track needs **at least 3 connection points**. Maximum
distances, from Appendix A of the Vancare guide:

| Lift capacity | Track: between supports | Track: cantilever | Super Track: between / cantilever | TrackPlus: between / cantilever |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 450 lb (C-450, P-440) | 86 in | 15 in | 147 / 34 in | 248 / 44 in |
| 600 lb (P-600) | 69 in | 13 in | 126 / 29 in | 216 / 38 in |
| 625 lb (C-625) | 66 in | 13 in | 124 / 29 in | 210 / 37 in |

Curves are shorter: 33 in (90°) and 56 in (45°) between supports at
450 lb. See the guide for the other capacities.

## 6. After the install: test and inspect

A trained tech does this before first use, with a client representative
watching (Vancare "Final Inspection"):

- Track level, end stops in place (bolts 12–14 ft-lb), set screws with
  blue Loctite 243 at 40–45 in-lb, end caps on. Run a lift through every
  joint, turntable and gate.
- Shake the track: supports and ends should barely move.
- **Load test at 150% of SWL:** roll a trolley carrying the weight on a
  chain hoist along the whole track (675 lb for a C-450).
- **Function test at 100% of SWL** on the carry bar: run the whole track
  and lift the weight 20 in off the floor.
- **Deflection test at 100% of SWL**, midway between supports: the track
  may sink no more than **span ÷ 200** (0.48 in on a 96 in span). If it
  sinks more, add or move supports.
- Check the lift: stops at the top and bottom, stops on a slack strap,
  emergency lowering works, every hand-control button works, and the
  charger reads 24–28 V at the contacts.
- Put the SWL label on the track.

**Repeat it every year (preventive maintenance)**, and any time the
system is overloaded or the structure, lift or track is changed. Take an
overloaded system out of service until it's inspected. C-Series lifts
also ask for maintenance after 1,000 lifts or 5 hours of running (see
[[C-450-C-625]]).
