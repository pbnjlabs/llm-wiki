# Brake Replacement

Golden's scooter/power-wheelchair drive motors all use the same category of
component — an electromagnetic parking/drive brake bolted to the motor
face — but the swap procedure's complexity varies a lot by chassis design,
from a 5-step handle-and-puck swap to a 28-step full drive-assembly pull.
Three model families have their own documented procedure; no single
"generic Golden brake replacement" exists, and the differences below are
real, not just documentation style.

Sources: [[2-Piece Brake Replacement (LiteRider, Buzzaround XL, Buzzaround Lite)]],
[[Brake Replacement (GL110, GL140)]],
[[Compass Sport Brake Replacement (GP605)]].

## LiteRider / Buzzaround XL / Buzzaround Lite (GL110, GL140, GB106, GB116, GB146)

The simplest case, and the one clear cross-model share: LiteRider and both
Buzzaround lines use the **same electromagnetic brake body**, differing
only in handle shape. See [[GL110-140-LiteRider]] and
[[GB1xx-Buzzaround-XL]].

- **[[2-Piece Brake Replacement (LiteRider, Buzzaround XL, Buzzaround Lite)]]**
  (2011-11-30) — the standard like-for-like swap: unbolt 4 screws from the
  motor, mount the new brake in freewheel position (12 o'clock), attach the
  model-specific handle, re-bolt. 5 steps, no teardown.
- **[[Brake Replacement (GL110, GL140)]]** (2011-03-15) — a different,
  earlier document for GL110/GL140 specifically: a **retrofit**, not a
  like-for-like swap. It replaces an old rearward-facing brake with a
  new-style upward-facing freewheel lever (more convenient to reach),
  includes drivetrain half-separation and a wheel pull, and swaps the
  handle color red→yellow for visibility. ~15 min, 2 pages.
  - **Not a contradiction**: this is an earlier-generation upgrade bulletin
    (2011-03-15) that predates the general 2-piece replacement procedure
    (2011-11-30). Read together, they suggest the upward-facing freewheel
    lever became the new-style standard, and the later 2-piece document
    describes replacing that new-style brake in kind. If servicing an
    unfamiliar GL110/GL140 in the field, check which lever orientation is
    already installed before picking a procedure — an old-style rearward
    lever needs the retrofit steps (half-separation, wheel pull, handle
    swap), not just the 5-step puck swap.

## Compass Sport (GP605)

By far the most involved: **[[Compass Sport Brake Replacement (GP605)]]**
(2011-02-18) is a 28-step, ~30-minute, per-side procedure — see
[[GP6xx-Compass]]. Covers/shrouds/seat post come off, factory tie-wraps are
cut, the M1/M2 motor harness is unplugged, the chair is supported so wheels
clear the ground, snap rings pulled, and the **entire drive assembly is
slid out on its pivot shafts** before the 4-screw brake itself can be
swapped — then everything reverses with side-specific harness routing and
tie-wrap counts. Also the procedure that confirms GP605's controller is
branded **"SHARK"** (see [[GP6xx-Compass]]), distinct from the PG VR2/LiNX
pair ([[GP162-LiteRider-Envy]]) and Dynamics R-Series
([[GC240-340-440-540-Companion]], [[GL110-140-LiteRider]]).

## Why the gap between them

The LiteRider/Buzzaround brake sits directly on an exposed motor face
reachable after a simple drivetrain split. The Compass Sport's brake is
buried inside a fuller chassis shroud, requiring the whole drive assembly
to be pulled on its pivot shafts to reach the same 4 screws. Same
component category (electromagnetic motor-face brake, 4-screw mount,
2-pin connector, verify-freewheel-in-neutral check), very different
chassis access — don't assume one family's time/step estimate applies to
another.
