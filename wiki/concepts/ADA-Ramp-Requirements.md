# ADA Ramp Requirements

The 2010 ADA Standards for ramps, taken from the U.S. Access Board's own
guide ([[ADA Technical Guide - Ramps and Curb Ramps]], July 2015). Page
numbers below refer to that PDF. The earlier EZ-ACCESS blog summary
([[Understanding ADA Ramp Requirements]]) is kept only for its corrections
listed at the bottom.

> **House standard still applies:** every access ramp is **1:12
> (4.8°)**, even where ADA allows steeper for alterations (see below).
> The only exception is temporary-use [[SUITCASE-Ramps]]. See
> [[Ramp-Slope-and-Length]].

## When a ramp is required (§303.4, p. 1)

- Any change in level **over ½ in** on an accessible route needs a
  ramp or curb ramp. An elevator or, in some cases, a platform lift can
  replace it.
- Any route section with a running slope **over 5 %** (1:20) counts as a
  ramp and must meet the ramp rules.

## Ramp runs (§405, pp. 1–2)

| Item | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Running slope | **1:12 max** (8.33 %), uniform along the run, with no grade breaks within a run |
| Alterations (space limited) | 1:10 max for a rise of 6 in or less; 1:8 max for 3 in or less. **Not used by PMS**: 1:12 house standard |
| Cross slope | **1:48 max** |
| Rise per run | **30 in max**. No limit on the number of runs |
| Clear width | **36 in min**, measured between the leading edges of the handrails. No maximum, and no center rail required |
| Surface | Firm, stable, slip-resistant (runs and landings) |
| Recommended | For exterior ramps, specify a 7.5 % running slope and a 1.5 % cross slope to absorb construction tolerances |

## Landings (§405.7, pp. 3–4)

- **Level landings at the top and bottom of every run.** A landing may
  slope no more than 1:48, which is enough for drainage.
- **Where a ramp changes direction, the landing must be at least 60 ×
  60 in clear.** Handrails, posts and edge protection cannot intrude
  into that space.
- Grade breaks must be perpendicular to the run.
- Door maneuvering clearance may overlap a landing. Keeping the door
  swing off the landing is recommended.
- Landings exposed to weather must not collect water (§405.10).
- Curved ramps without level landings usually fail because of compound
  slopes (p. 17).

## Handrails (§405.8, §505, pp. 5–6)

- **Required on both sides when the rise is over 6 in.**
- **Height 34–38 in**, the same along the whole run.
- **12 in min extensions**, level, at the top and bottom of each run.
  Extensions must return to a guard, wall or floor. The inside rail of
  a switchback or dogleg can be continuous instead.
- Continuous for the full run. Up to 20 % of the underside may be
  obstructed; the top and sides may not.
- Circular rail **1¼–2 in** in diameter. Non-circular: 4–6¼ in
  perimeter, 2¼ in max cross-section.
- **1½ in min** clearance to a wall.
- Optional children's rail: 28 in max height, 9 in min below the main
  rail.
- A handrail may be mounted on a guard rail.

## Edge protection (§405.9, p. 8)

Use one of the following:

- A curb at least **4 in** high.
- A barrier or pickets that stop a **4 in sphere**.
- A run or landing surface that extends **12 in** beyond the inside
  face of the handrail.

Not required on ramps 6 in high or less that have side flares, on
landing sides that serve an adjoining run or stairway, or where the
drop-off is ½ in or less within 10 in of the landing.

## Portable ramps (p. 17)

"All required ramps and curb ramps must be permanent." Only ramps that
serve temporary structures may be temporary or portable. This lines up
with PMS treating [[GATEWAY-3G]] and [[PATHWAY-3G]] as permanent and
limiting [[SUITCASE-Ramps]] to temporary use.

## Curb ramps (§406, pp. 9–16), in brief

Running slope 1:12 max (alterations: the same 1:10 and 1:8 exceptions),
cross slope 1:48, counter slope at the gutter 1:20, flares 1:10 max, a
top landing at least 36 in deep, parallel curb ramp landings at least
48 in (60 in preferred), and 48 in between ramps on islands. Detectable
warnings are required only at DOT transit facilities. This applies to
sidewalk and site work, which PMS rarely does.

## PMS handbook vs. ADA

The PMS [[Ramps Handbook]] frames its five design rules as "ADA
guidelines". They mostly match this guide. Differences:

| Topic | PMS handbook | ADA (this guide) |
|---|---|---|
| Slope | 1:12, slope meter ≤ 4.8° | 1:12 max (1:10/1:8 allowed in some alterations) |
| Landings | 60 in level at top and bottom of every run and at the door, **as wide as the ramp** | Level at top and bottom; **60 × 60 in** where the ramp changes direction |
| Run limit | 30 ft of continuous ramp without a rest | 30 in max rise per run (the same thing at 1:12) |
| Handrails | **At least one** continuous handrail | **Both sides** when the rise is over 6 in; 34–38 in high; 12 in extensions |

The handbook's single-handrail minimum is below ADA's two-sided rule.
That is fine for a private home, where ADA doesn't apply, but not for a
public or commercial job. PATHWAY runs normally ship with a handrail
pair per section anyway (see [[PATHWAY-3G]]).

## Does it apply to homes?

ADA governs **public and commercial** facilities, not private homes. PMS
applies the slope rule everywhere anyway (house standard). For the
rest, the ADA numbers are good defaults for a residential job unless
local code says otherwise: 36 in width, landings at the top and bottom,
60 × 60 in turning landings, rails on both sides above 6 in of rise,
34–38 in rail height. The [[PATHWAY-3G]] manual itself refers to ADA
once: its platform tie strap is limited to a ½ in gap when the ramp is
installed "per ADA Guidelines."

Commercial product: [[PATHWAY-HD]] (Code Compliant Modular Access
System, 48 in usable width).

## Corrections to the EZ-ACCESS blog summary

[[Understanding ADA Ramp Requirements]] is broadly right, but:

- It says a landing is needed "every 30 ft of run." The actual rule is
  **30 in max rise per run**. The two match only at exactly 1:12.
- It gives 60 × 60 in as the size of every landing. The guide requires
  60 × 60 in **where the ramp changes direction**. Top and bottom
  landings must be level; their length isn't given in this guide.
- Its clipping is missing the handrail and edge-protection rules, which
  are now covered above.
