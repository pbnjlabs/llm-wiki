# GATEWAY 3G Instructions and Technical Specifications

EZ-ACCESS's 13-page instructions for the **GATEWAY 3G Solid Surface
Portable Ramp** (doc **17838 REV 08-02-2023**) and its 3-page spec sheet
(doc **18524 REV 07-11-2024**). The first manufacturer documents for the
GATEWAY in the wiki; until now [[GATEWAY-3G]] relied on clippings.

## Covers

- **1,000 lb**, 6000-series aluminum, **residential use only**. Surface
  is extruded or SAFEGUARD extra-fine direct gritting. Self-adjusting
  lower transition plate, fixed top lip plate (the end with two holes).
  Made in the USA. Lifetime limited warranty, **powder coat excluded**.
- **Handrails are required if anyone walks on the ramp.** Three styles:
  ramp only, Two-Line handrails (TL), Vertical Pickets (VP).

- **Size:** usable width 36 in; overall 37 in wide and 3.5 in high (5.5
  in on the 9 and 10 ft). With handrails: 41.75 in overall width, 36 in
  overall height (34.5 in usable); each handrail is 1 ft shorter than the
  ramp.
- **EZ-ACCESS's rise guide is steeper than 1:12** (e.g. 9 in on a 6 ft
  ramp is about 1:8). PMS treats the GATEWAY as permanent, so **the 1:12
  house standard applies**: the PMS column is 1 in per foot (wiki
  arithmetic).
- **Tools** (p. 3): 1/2 in socket or wrench, 1/4 in nut driver, drill,
  #2 Phillips, 1/4 in masonry bit (concrete), construction adhesive.
- **Handrails** (§4): lay the ramp face down on cardboard or grass. Slide
  each of the 4 end brackets' upper lip into the side-rail groove and
  bolt it through its innermost hole into the threaded insert (5/16-18 ×
  1-1/2 in bolt and washer, 1/2 in socket). Turn the ramp over without
  hitting the studs. Set each post's two holes on the bracket studs, 2
  washers and 2 nuts per post. Rails swap side to side; if the holes
  don't line up, check the bracket is in the right insert, or flip the
  rail end for end.
- **End loops** (§5): the **upper loop is larger** (plate angles in,
  longer from bend to plate); the lower is smaller (plate angles out).
  They aren't interchangeable. O-ring over the swaged end against the
  shoulder, one 1/4 in self-drilling hex screw through the plate, and one
  #10 × 1/2 in self-drilling Phillips screw about 1 in from the end on
  the underside of the rail (punch a divot or pre-drill 1/8 in first;
  hand-tighten until no thread shows).
- **Setup** (§6): the top lip plate must fully overlap a level landing
  and rest firmly against it. Anchor before each use: **clevis pins** in
  drilled 1/4 in holes (temporary), or installer-supplied hardware set
  flush (semi-permanent). Glue the 4 **corner caps** onto the side-rail
  ends with construction adhesive.
- **Final checks** (§7): all fasteners tight, walk the ramp for movement,
  level and slope unchanged, loops on every rail end. Touch up scratches
  on architectural-finish rails with 180-grit along the grain; **no
  abrasives on paint or powder coat**.
- **Deicing** (§9): magnesium chloride works to −13 °F and doesn't harm
  concrete or metal (moderately harmful to plants); calcium chloride (5 °F) and salt (18 °F) damage
  concrete and metal and harm plants; potassium chloride (25 °F) is OK on
  old concrete; CMA (25 °F) is the gentlest. Salt and calcium chloride
  are worst on new concrete, brick and stone. (Table from University of
  Maryland Fact Sheet 707.)
- Don't attach anything to the ramp (planters, lights, decorations).
  Aluminum conducts electricity: stay off it in electrical storms.
- **Incline chart** (p. 12): image only, like the SUITCASE chart.
