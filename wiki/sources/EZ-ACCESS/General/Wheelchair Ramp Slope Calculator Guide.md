# Wheelchair Ramp Slope Calculator Guide

EZ-ACCESS blog article (2026-07-11), clipped from the web. Largely
overlaps [[How Long Should a Wheelchair Ramp Be]] (same author, published
two months earlier) but adds rise-measurement technique, ramp
dimensions beyond length, and an aluminum-vs-wood comparison.

## Covers

- Rise × 12 = length in inches (1:12); how slope affects wheelchair users
  vs. caregivers.
- **Rise-to-length table** (1:12): 12 in → 12 ft, 17-1/4 in → 18 ft,
  24 in → 24 ft, 29-1/2 in → 30 ft, 36 in → 36 ft. Disagrees slightly
  with the sister article's table at 12 and 24 ft — see
  [[Ramp-Slope-and-Length]].
- Measuring rise correctly: ground-to-entry-point, not one step; ground
  sloping away from the house lengthens the ramp; measure available run
  space.
- Dimensions to check: length, width (room for a helper), landings/
  turning space, door swing and threshold height at the top.
- Modular vs. portable vs. threshold ramps, naming [[PATHWAY-3G]],
  [[GATEWAY-3G]], [[SUITCASE-Ramps]], and [[TRANSITIONS-Threshold-Ramps]].
- Wood ramps: sealing/staining, warping/cracking/sagging; aluminum:
  corrosion-resistant, low maintenance.
- Calculator mistakes: wrong rise point, forgetting footprint, ignoring
  landings/door clearance, choosing on length alone.
