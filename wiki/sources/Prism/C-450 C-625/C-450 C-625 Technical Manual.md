# C-450/C-625 Technical Manual

45-page combined technical manual (troubleshooting, repairs, replacements)
for the **C-450/C-625 ceiling lift**, Rev 25 Apr 2018. A field-service
manual, not an operator/owner manual — assumes a technician with basic
hand tools disassembling the lift.

Structure:
- **Symptoms and Problems** (pp.3–10): "before getting inside" checklist,
  then a systems-based diagnostic walkthrough — pneumatic, electrical,
  mechanical — for a technician narrowing down a fault before opening the
  cover.
- **Instruction sheets** (pp.11–28): numbered step-by-step repair
  procedures, each with photos — B1 (cover access), P1–P3 (pneumatic:
  airline, grommet, PCB air switches), E1–E8 (electrical: batteries,
  combined PCB, charger adaptor board, charger/end stop, charger contact
  strips, constant charger system, up/down micro switch assembly,
  emergency shut-off), M1–M3 (mechanical: lifting strap, trolley wheels,
  traversing drive motor/gears).
- **LCD Display & Programming Functionality** (pp.29–33): end-user display
  modes and a technician programming-mode menu (traversing speed,
  preventative-maintenance alarm, lift/hour counters, return-to-charge
  settings) reached via simultaneous Up/Down button holds.
- **Tool List** (p.34) and **Service Parts List** (pp.35–45): exploded-view
  diagrams with part numbers, organized by assembly (cover, battery/board,
  traverse gearing, constant charge, left plate, motor plate, tape gear,
  bottom plate, charger endstop, low-height carry bar).

Covers three drivetrain configurations sharing one combined PCB design (2
air switches = Manual; 4 = Power or Manual-PT; 6 = Power X-Y or Power
Turntable) — the number of air switches on the PCB is itself a quick way
to identify which configuration a given lift is.

Notably, this manual documents the **same class of overspeed governor**
used on Bruno's straight stairlifts (see [[Overspeed-Safety-Brake]]) — a
centrifugal-force mechanical brake, independent of the electro-pneumatic
system, that halts the lift if the motor/worm-wheel wears out and causes
overspeed. Unlike Bruno's field-resettable version, the manual describes
this as requiring motor replacement, not a reset procedure.
