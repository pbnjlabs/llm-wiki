# Video: How to Replace the Actuator (AL-100)

Harmar training video, 9:10, presented by Harmar's Director of
Engineering. A full **remove-and-install** of the motor actuator on an
outside (hitch) lift, done **on the vehicle**. The unit is an
**AL-100-12** (mast label; serial MMOAL161026-00020, so probably an
October 2016 build) on a Dodge Grand Caravan. No video date. The
narrator says it applies to "the outside lift products". Feeds
[[AL-Series-Outside-Vehicle-Lifts]].

Ingested from a Whisper transcript (`medium.en`) plus frames every 10 s;
times below are video timestamps.

## Which actuator

The demo lift was built before Harmar's Aug 2019 change from actuator
**ALA10010** to **310-00005** ([[Harmar Parts Diagrams]]), so it most
likely shows the ALA10010, the same one as
[[Actuator Removal Instructions (ALA10010-INS rev 2)]]. The video
doesn't give a part number for the actuator or the tool. The
310-00005 drawing ([[Actuator Drawing 310-00005 (Rev C)]]) says it's the ALA10010 renumbered, with
internal changes only, and its ball-nut test seats the nut with its sides
parallel to the nut plate, about ¼–½ turn short of fully seated, which
matches this video's install step.

## Tools (said in the video)

- **5/16" bit driver** on an extension, on a cordless drill in reverse,
  or a wrench (slower), for the actuator-to-tube screws
- **7/16" socket**, ratchet and extension (or nut driver) for the
  locking-tab bolt; a **magnetic socket**, or grease/tape in the socket
  to hold the bolt
- **Flat-blade screwdriver** for the locking tab
- **Ball nut tool**: "basically a piece of channel", **supplied with the
  replacement actuator** (the sheet calls it ALA10010-TOOL); channel
  locks or a crescent wrench for extra leverage if needed
- **Jack stand** to hold the lift up

## Removal

| Time | Step |
|---|---|
| 0:11 | Pre-steps for the demo: platform removed (only so the camera can see) and the motor cover bolts already out. |
| 0:24 | Done **on the vehicle**, "probably the way that it's going to be most common". |
| 0:35 | With the inner arm in the **upper position, block it**: a jack stand under the lower arm. **When the actuator comes out the lower tube drops**, so without support "you're going to have something fall on you." |
| 1:03 | Remove the motor cover and unplug the motor. |
| 1:14 | **Screws under the actuator: 5/16".** The narrator says four, then removes **three**; the sheet says the 4th is slotted and stays. They're about **2 in long, not fully threaded**, and must come all the way out. |
| 1:39–2:41 | Order: the side screw, then the back one, and **the tightest one last**, at an angle. |
| 2:43 | With the three out, the actuator is free of the tubes. |
| 3:05 | Back the actuator off a little, by hand on the manual override screw, to make room for tools inside the tube. (The sheet says raise the motor about 8–12 in.) |
| 3:34 | **Locking-tab bolt, 7/16"**, down inside the tube. Grease in the socket keeps the bolt from dropping (4:04). |
| 4:30 | Pry the **locking tab** up with a flat-blade screwdriver and fish it out (4:54 shows the tab and screw). |
| 5:07 | **Unscrew the ball nut** with the ball nut tool. It **should turn by hand**. |
| 5:35 | Near the top, **support the actuator** and slide it straight out. The threaded hole is visible inside (5:49). |

## Installation (not on the sheet)

| Time | Step |
|---|---|
| 5:53 | Reverse the process: drop the end of the new screw through the threaded hole in the inner tube. |
| 6:09 | **Thread the ball nut in by hand** with the tool. **If it gets hard to turn, it's cross-threaded or misaligned**; it should thread easily. Run it down to **hand tight**. |
| 6:50 | Check that the ball nut is **seated on the bottom**. |
| 6:56 | **Back the ball nut up until its square sides are parallel to the tube walls**, so the locking tab fits. |
| 7:17 | Put the **locking tab between the ball nut and the outer tube**, centered. |
| 7:26 | Reinstall the 7/16" bolt **flush and tight**. **The locking tab and screw stop the ball nut coming loose; if the ball nut comes loose, the lift isn't supported.** |
| 8:23 | Check everything is snug and flush at the bottom. |
| 8:27 | Drive the actuator back down and reattach it to the tubes with the **three screws**. **Check there are no gaps around the actuator's perimeter**: a gap misaligns it and can make the unit clutch. Torque them down (no value given). |
| 8:49 | Motor cover back on (**4 screws**), **zip tie the motor wire** at the back. Reconnect it. |
