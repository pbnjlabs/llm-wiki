# Actuator Removal Instructions (ALA10010-INS rev 2)

One-page Harmar instruction sheet, drawings only, for removing the
actuator from a hitch-mounted (outside) vehicle lift. It's a scanned,
image-only PDF, printed sideways; there is **no revision date**. Feeds
[[AL-Series-Outside-Vehicle-Lifts]].

## Which actuator it covers

The sheet number is **ALA10010-INS**, and **ALA10010** is Harmar's
older actuator part number. Per the assembly drawings in
[[Harmar Parts Diagrams]], both the AL100 (rev H, 13AUG19,
ECO-004479) and the AL301XL replaced **ALA10010 with 310-00005**. So the
sheet is written for the pre-August-2019 actuator. Whether the current
310-00005 comes out the same way isn't stated; the drawings show it in
the same position. Confirm with Harmar (800-833-0478) before relying on
it for a newer lift. The pictured lift is an outside platform lift with a
hold-down arm (AL100 style).

## Procedure (from the drawings)

Tools shown: **3/8" socket** (manual override hex shaft), **7/16"
wrench** (retaining bolt), and Harmar's ball-nut tool **ALA10010-TOOL**.

1. **Get the lift on the ground.** Preferred: remove the lift from the
   vehicle and set it on the ground. Optional: leave it on the vehicle
   with the lift in the **UP** position, **braced firmly from
   underneath** (blocks under the platform).
2. **Remove the motor cover** (4 screws). **Collapse the inner and outer
   tube**, either with power and the toggle switch (push **UP**) or by
   turning the manual override (3/8" on the hex shaft). With the
   platform on the ground, **remove 3 bolts** at the top of the outer
   tube. The 4th bolt is slotted and doesn't need to come out.
3. **Hold the motor** and **raise it off the outer tube about 8–12 in**,
   with power and the toggle (push **DOWN**) or the manual override. The
   fine print says the inner tube/platform must be firmly planted for the
   motor to separate (partly illegible on the scan).
4. **Remove the retaining bolt** (7/16") and the **locking tab**. Use
   **ALA10010-TOOL** to **unscrew the ball nut**, lining up the open side
   of the tool with the **ball return tube**. **Pull the actuator
   straight up and out.**

The sheet covers removal only. Installation isn't shown; presumably the
reverse, so check the ball return tube lines up with the tool slot again
going back in.

## Before replacing an actuator

Confirm the actuator is actually bad. Per the 2026-10-14 field note, a
lift that sticks at **the same spot only under load** is a worn J-cam,
not the actuator. An actuator or power problem is **slow the whole way
up**. A ratchet at the top of travel is the actuator clutch working
normally ([[AL-Series-Outside-Vehicle-Lifts]] operation notes).
