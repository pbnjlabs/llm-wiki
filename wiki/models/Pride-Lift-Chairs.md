# Pride Lift Chairs

Pride power lift chairs and recliners. PMS sells them (a 2022 private-pay
Pride lift chair sale is in [[Delivery Ticket and Invoice Examples]]);
**all lift chair sales are final** ([[PMS-Office-Procedures]]).

> **Missing manuals.** Only Pride's 2008 basic troubleshooting sheet is
> on file: no owner's manual, technical troubleshooting guide or
> installation instructions for any Pride lift chair model. When working
> on one, get the manual for that model from Pride (pridemobility.com,
> or Pride Technical Service **1-877-800-1248**, then 7 and 5 for lift
> chair tech).

- [[Lift Chair Basic Troubleshooting (INFINFB2061)]] (Rev A, 2008).
  **Not for models with Okin motors.**

## Before troubleshooting

1. Transformer plugged into a live, **non-switched** outlet; test the
   outlet.
2. Every connection plugged in and locked; reconnect everything,
   power included, while testing.
3. Cables free and not pinched by the mechanism; power cable from
   control box to transformer not crimped, cut or cracked.
4. **Dip switch** in the standard control box set correctly, or the
   hand control buttons run the wrong things (motors moving together,
   heat/massage running a motor).
5. Replacing a motor: fit the new motor bottom/base first, chair fully
   reclined if possible.

## Symptom → check

- **Stopped / clicks but won't move:** transformer, motor, hand control,
  motor relay box, control box.
- **Drops, or drops suddenly and quits:** broken **motor clevis or
  stroke tube**, bent or broken scissors or lift frame, cracked
  lift/recline bracket, broken side bracket pin, T-bracket catching.
  Keep people out of the chair until it's found.
- **Won't lift or recline all the way, sits crooked, one side lands
  first:** **dual motors out of timing**, bent scissors, twisted wood
  frame, leveling leg, uneven floor, loose bolts, worn seat foam.
- **Footrest won't open or close fully:** side brackets, frame
  adjustment, motor or stroke tube, footrest mounting height, fabric
  catching.
- **Squeaks / pops:** scissors, loose or overtight bolts, lift frame
  rubbing the wood frame, T-bracket.
- **Upholstery** (wrinkles, bunching, popping springs or buttons):
  foam, fabric, chaise pad position on its fasteners, spring clips,
  button string. Getting in and out without using the hand control
  separates the arm wood from the seat base.
