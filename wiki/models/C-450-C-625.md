# C-450 / C-625

Overhead track-mounted patient ceiling lift from Prism Medical, pneumatically
operated (hand-control airline) with an electro-mechanical drive. Three
build configurations exist, distinguished by the number of air switches on
the shared combined PCB:

**Two further "C-Series" siblings exist but are undocumented**: C-800
(800 lb capacity) and C-1000 (1000 lb capacity), confirmed only via
[[Vancare]]'s ceiling-lift track installation guide (its hand-control
parts tables and charger compatibility list both name all four C-Series
models) — no manual of their own is on file. See also [[P440]], Prism's
other documented product line (a portable, non-track-fixed ceiling
lift).

| Power or Manual Power Turntable (PT) | 4 |

## Pre-install site survey

Before installing, [[Ceiling Lift Evaluation Form]] captures patient specs
(weight/height, amputee status), intended lift use (bed-to-chair only vs.
bed-to-chair-and-bathtub), and a structural assessment that branches by
house type:

- **Site-built home**: check for attic space directly above the install
  room(s) and whether another room sits above, for ceiling-track mounting.
- **Mobile home**: different requirements — transportable motors with
  wall-mounted track instead of standard ceiling track, plus confirming
  attic access before assuming standard joist-mount hardware works.

Note: the form's example track-layout diagram uses a **Handicare** brand
component legend, not Prism — Performance Medical Supply's site-survey
process/track hardware may not be exclusive to this product line.

## How it works

Three subsystems, diagnosed in this order per the manual: **pneumatic**
(hand control → airline → grommet → PCB air switches — controls on/off,
up/down, and traverse), **electrical** (batteries → combined PCB/charger
adaptor board → wire harnesses → motors — batteries read via LCD, DC
voltage should be >27.5V charged / 27–30V while charging), **mechanical**
(lifting strap, tape switch assembly with slack-strap and thick-strap
safety limit switches, trolley wheels, traversing motor/gears).

## Safety mechanisms

- **Slack-strap / thick-strap limit switches**: a tape-switch roller
  assembly detects an over-extended or doubled-over strap and reverses
  up/down function as a fail-safe — if this is stuck or misaligned, "lift
  goes down then reverses direction" is the symptom.
- **Overspeed governor**: a centrifugal mechanical brake independent of the
  pneumatic/electrical system, described as "universal, failsafe." Triggers
  if the motor output shaft or worm wheel wears out and the lift
  accelerates abnormally; per this manual, recovery requires **motor
  replacement**, not a field reset. See [[Overspeed-Safety-Brake]] for the
  cross-manufacturer comparison (Bruno's stairlift version of the same
  concept is field-resettable).
- **Emergency Shut-Off with pull cord**: cuts battery power entirely (pull
  and release) or, held at full extension, activates emergency lowering.
  Can only be reset by a qualified technician after inspection.

## LCD / programming

Default display shows either battery level or lifetime lift count; drops
to a flashing "Low Battery" warning under 25% charge, and always shows a
"Charging" override while docked. Technician **programming mode** (hold
Up+Down 3 sec) exposes: display mode, traversing speed (2/4/6/8, factory
6), preventative-maintenance alarm on/off, lift/hour counters (and reset),
and — on lifts with the return-to-charge (RTC) feature — RTC max time,
drop time, and speed. Preventative maintenance is recommended after 1,000
lifts or 5 total operating hours; the lift beeps and flashes "Maintenance"
until the counter is reset following the manual's Semi-Annual PM procedure.

## Known quirks/gotchas

- The circuit board is described as non-serviceable at the component
  level — any board-level fault (blown fuse aside) means whole-board
  replacement, confirmed by swapping in a known-good board before
  committing to the repair.
- Charging strips were originally copper (corrodes in humid/pool
  environments) and have been superseded by stainless steel on the newer
  lift design — a tech seeing copper strips on an older unit should expect
  more frequent contact corrosion issues.
- Polarity matters throughout: battery leads, charger end-stop wiring
  (red = positive), and motor jumper-testing (reversed polarity runs the
  motor backward) are all called out explicitly as failure points if
  swapped.
