# PATHWAY 3G Modular Access System

EZ-ACCESS's **residential** modular aluminum ramp system. Ramp sections,
platforms and handrails are combined into straight, L, U or switchback
layouts to reach high porches and entries. PMS treats it as permanent,
and every run is built at **1:12 (4.8°)** (see [[Ramp-Slope-and-Length]]).
For the commercial counterpart, see [[PATHWAY-HD]].

## Generation note: check before quoting specs

The only manual on file is the **2015 "Pathway®" Assembly Manual**
([[Pathway Modular Access System Assembly Manual]], rev 05-12-15). It
never says "3G". The web articles (2021–2026) all say "PATHWAY 3G", so
the manual most likely documents the earlier generation. Where they
differ:

| Spec | 2015 Pathway manual | PATHWAY 3G articles |
|---|---|---|
| Weight capacity | **850 lb**, residential use only | 1,000 lb ([[Different Types of Threshold Ramps]], 2021) |

Until a 3G manual turns up, use the manual for **assembly procedures**,
since the modular design is the same family. For **capacity**, confirm
which generation is on the job. When in doubt, quote 850 lb.

## Installation essentials (manual §2–5)

- **Tools**: ½ in and 9/16 in sockets, 5/32 in and 3/16 in Allen
  wrenches (both included), ¼ in masonry bit (for concrete), 1/8 in and
  5/16 in bits, level, rubber mallet, hacksaw (for handrail
  connectors), 25 ft tape. **Two installers.** Check the packing list
  first.
- **Order of work**: all platforms and ramps first, handrails last.
  Stay off platforms until every set screw is tight, and off ramps until
  installation is complete.
- **Platforms**: tip the platform on its side to insert the support
  tubes. Snug one set screw per corner, level the platform, then tighten
  the second set screw. The feet point under the platform. On **soft
  soil**, set each foot on a concrete pad.
- **Bracing** (platforms and ramps): walking surface **over 36 in**
  high needs angle braces (MUAB). **72 in or more** needs cross braces
  as well.
- **Ramp runs**: assemble upside down on cardboard or grass. Join
  sections butted with no gap, using 2 saddle brackets per joint and
  8 bolts. **Join at most 3 sections at a time.** Hang the run on MRHP
  hangers, set the support legs, and raise each joint to take out sag.
  Sight down the run so the sections stay parallel, or the handrails
  won't fit.
- **Handrail bracket gotcha**: if the handrail posts miss the studs by
  6 in, the end bracket is in the wrong threaded insert. It belongs in
  the insert **second from the end**.
- **Ground transition**: hook it into the end tread, rotate it down, and
  anchor it (wood or concrete hardware included). Use it at the ground
  end only. If the run ends on a landing pad, use the MRLT instead.
- **Upper transition anchoring**: on wood, use a ¼ in stainless
  self-drilling screw in each corner hole. On concrete, drill 1½ in deep
  with a ¼ in masonry bit, clean the holes, and drive the mushroom-head
  pin anchors.
- **Angling a ramp to a platform or porch**: this is a **plan-view
  (horizontal) angle**, not slope. The upper transition handles about
  4°. The lower transition plus MRST handles about 8°. Either one must
  overlap the surface by **at least 1 in**.
- **Handrail connectors**: cut the 1½ in round tube to fit on site.
  Measure the upper and lower rails separately, because their gaps
  differ. Clearances needed: ramp HR to ramp HR at least 6 in from the
  platform edge to each ramp side rail (8 in between side rails on a
  turn-back). Platform HR to ramp HR at least 10 in. On platforms wider
  than 5 ft, use two MHRPRC.
- **Final steps**: fasten **2 clips at every ramp/platform joint**
  (1/8 in pilot holes, ¼ × 1 in screws). Check that every fastener is
  tight, walk the system, clear metal chips, **re-check level and
  slope**, and cap or loop every handrail end.

## Optional equipment (manual §6)

- **Bridge plate** (MBP): flat position only, gaps up to **6 in**,
  at least 1 in of bearing on each side. Never use it as a ramp.
- **Platform tie strap** (MPTS): joins a platform to a porch, deck or
  threshold with little or no gap, **½ in max for an ADA install**.
- **Landing pad** (MLP): at the ramp bottom on soft or uneven ground,
  used with an MRLT.
- **Lightning ground rod** (MLGRK): aluminum conducts electricity.
  Drive the rod within 1½ ft of a support tube, leaving about 2 in
  exposed, with #8 copper wire to a brace band. **Locate utilities
  first.**
- **Gate** (MUG36): 36 in, swings either way, gravity latch.

## Storm tie-downs (manual §6.9)

The MTDK kit is a pair of auger anchors (for soil, or through asphalt)
plus wire rope and hardware. It's designed for hurricane wind and
seismic loads but **not rated for specific soils or seismic zones**.
Local code may require site-specific anchoring calculations stamped by
an engineer; EZ-ACCESS sales can arrange those.

- Place anchors in opposing pairs, one at **each platform corner
  tube**. On a single ramp, place them near its center, using eyebolts
  through 5/16 in holes in the side rails about ¾ in above the bottom of
  the rail.
- Brace band about 2 in below the corner pocket. Drive the auger
  **within 10° of vertical** until only the eye shows. Pull the rope
  taut, clamp it with 3/16 in clamps, and tape the cut ends.
- **Locate utilities first. Never put concrete anchors in asphalt**;
  go through the asphalt with auger anchors instead.
- Inspect regularly and re-tighten after storms (see also
  [[Securing PATHWAY 3G Before a Storm]]).

## Maintenance and safety (manual §7, give to the end user)

- Use the ramp only with a qualified helper, and always wear the
  mobility device's lap belt.
- Follow the device's Owner Guide for incline and chair direction.
- Check periodically for damage and loose fasteners. If anything is
  damaged or loose, **don't use it until a certified installer repairs
  it**.
- Clean by sweeping or hosing, or with mild soap and water. **Avoid
  alkaline detergents.**
- Clear ice and snow before use. Magnesium chloride is fine for melting,
  but wash the residue off afterward.
- No planters or decorations on the handrails. No playing on the ramp.
- Touch up scratches on architectural-finish rails with 180-grit
  sandpaper along the grain, never on painted or powder-coated rails.
